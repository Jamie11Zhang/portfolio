Building an Internal Platform to Simplify Team Workflows

Turning fragmented processes and repetitive tasks into a more connected way of working.

I initiated and built an internal platform to bring fragmented team workflows into one place. The platform brought together resource management, activity tracking, automated reminders, and weekly reporting.

I started by exploring how the team worked, identifying recurring pain points, and discussing requirements with colleagues and my team leader. I then used vibe coding to build and refine the platform, learning how to work with APIs and automate tasks along the way.

  • My contributionProposed the project, gathered requirements, designed the workflow, and built the platform myself through vibe coding
  • ContextAnt International · Internal marketing operations tool
  • MethodsRequirements gathering · Workflow mapping · Vibe coding · API integration · Workflow automation

The challenge

The team's marketing operations work ran across a lot of recurring tasks: monitoring market and competitor activity, managing partner resources, preparing for events, and handling post-event follow-ups.

Because this work lived in separate places, it was easy to lose track of tasks, and a lot of time went into repetitive manual steps. I saw a chance to pull these activities into one place and give the team a more consistent way to work.

Understanding the workflow

I started with my own assumptions about the pain points, but quickly found they weren't enough to design something that fit how people actually worked.

So I watched how the work happened, mapped out the individual steps, talked to colleagues, and worked through requirements with my team leader. I kept checking my thinking against their feedback as I built, which shifted the platform away from the process I'd imagined and towards the one the team needed.

Designing the solution

  • Market activity monitoring A dashboard for tracking notable market and competitor activities and sharing relevant updates with the Business Development (BD) team.
  • Resource library A centralised collection of partner, supplier, association, speaker, and merchandise resources.
  • Activity operations workspace To-do lists supporting both event preparation and post-event review.
  • Automated reminders and notifications Daily reminders and automated messages sent to individuals to support ongoing tasks.
  • Weekly reporting Automated generation of weekly reports to reduce recurring manual work.

The aim across all of these was the same: keep the information in one place, make tasks easier to track, and take the recurring work off people's plates.

Building the platform

This was the first time I'd built a platform through vibe coding, and I did the build myself. I worked turn by turn with an AI coding tool to go from requirements to working features, then kept refining them.

It's also where I picked up API integration and workflow automation in practice — connecting the platform to the data and tools it needed, and automating the recurring steps the team was doing by hand. A lot of that came from trial and error and a lot of back-and-forth with the tool.

I didn't take what the tool produced at face value. Each piece went through a loop of building, reviewing, testing, and fixing before it was something the team could rely on.

Outcomes

The platform pulled together several recurring activities that had been scattered before — activity monitoring, resource management, event task tracking, reminders, and weekly reporting — into a single place the team could use day to day.

For me, it was proof I could take a messy operational problem and turn it into something people actually used, while keeping what I built close to what they needed.

What I learned

The biggest takeaway was that a useful tool starts with the people and the process, not the build. My early assumptions got me moving, but it was the conversations with colleagues and my team leader that showed me where I was wrong and what the work actually looked like.

It also changed how I approach something technical that I don't yet know how to do. By learning through vibe coding and a lot of experimentation, I could build something real and pick up the skills as I went.

If I did it again, I'd bring users in earlier, test my assumptions before committing to a direction, and keep improving the platform based on how it actually got used.
