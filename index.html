Case study · Marketing operations

FMOP: Turning operational pain points into a workflow platform

An internal platform for marketing activity monitoring, resource management, and workflow automation.

I initiated and built FMOP, an internal platform designed to streamline marketing operations by bringing fragmented resources, activity management, and recurring tasks into a more connected workflow. Using AI-assisted development, I translated operational needs into practical tools, iterating on the platform as I learned more about how the team actually worked.

  • My contributionInitiated the project, ran requirements discovery, designed the workflow, and built the platform with AI-assisted development
  • ContextAnt International · Internal operations tool
  • MethodsRequirements discovery · Workflow mapping · APIs · AI-assisted development

The challenge

Marketing operations involved a range of recurring tasks, from monitoring market and competitor activities to managing partner resources, coordinating event preparation, and completing post-event follow-ups.

Information and processes were spread across different activities, while repetitive manual work made it harder to keep track of tasks and maintain a consistent workflow. I saw an opportunity to bring these activities together in a more structured and efficient way.

Understanding the workflow

My initial approach was to identify potential pain points and form hypotheses about what the team needed. However, I learned that assumptions alone were not enough to design a solution that fit real working practices.

I observed existing workflows, mapped individual steps, spoke with colleagues, and discussed requirements with my team leader. I also gathered feedback during development and used it to refine the solution. This process helped me move beyond designing around my own assumptions and towards understanding the needs of the people who would use the platform.

Designing the solution

  • Market activity monitoring A dashboard for tracking notable market and competitor activities and sharing relevant updates with the Business Development (BD) team.
  • Resource library A centralised collection of partner, supplier, association, speaker, and merchandise resources.
  • Activity operations workspace To-do lists supporting both event preparation and post-event review.
  • Automated reminders and notifications Daily reminders and automated messages sent to individuals to support ongoing tasks.
  • Weekly reporting Automated generation of weekly reports to reduce recurring manual work.

Together, these capabilities were designed to make operational information easier to access, tasks easier to track, and recurring processes less dependent on manual follow-up.

Building with AI

This was my first hands-on experience building a platform through vibe coding. I used an AI coding assistant throughout the development process, working iteratively to translate requirements into functionality and refine the implementation.

I also learned how to work with APIs to support workflow automation. Through extensive experimentation and repeated conversations with the AI assistant, I developed a more practical understanding of how to connect tools and turn an idea into a working solution.

Rather than treating AI-generated output as a finished product, I used it as part of an iterative process of building, reviewing, testing, and improving.

Outcomes

FMOP brought several recurring marketing operations activities into one platform, combining activity monitoring, resource management, event task tracking, reminders, and reporting. The project also gave me hands-on experience translating operational problems into a working digital solution, while learning to balance implementation with the needs of the people using it.

What I learned

The most important lesson was that building a useful tool starts with understanding the people and processes behind the problem. My initial assumptions helped me get started, but conversations with colleagues and feedback from my team leader showed me why solutions need to reflect real workflows rather than an imagined ideal process.

The project also changed how I approach unfamiliar technical challenges. By learning through experimentation and AI-assisted development, I was able to build something practical while developing new technical skills along the way.

Going forward, I would involve users even earlier, validate assumptions before committing to a solution, and continue refining the platform based on how it is used in practice.
